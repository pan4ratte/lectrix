# 6.1 Viewer

Part of the Lectrix specification. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and [README.md](README.md) lists these files; sections keep the numbers they share, so "section 5.4" is in AGENTS.md and "section 6.5" in [annotations.md](annotations.md).

- **Opening:** File > Open dialog, drag-and-drop onto the window, `.pdf` file association, and a path passed on the command line. On macOS, files from Finder, Open With or another app come as an open event (`RunEvent::Opened`), at launch or while Lectrix runs, and are opened as a second launch's files are (`hand_over` in `src-tauri/src/lib.rs`); files handed over before the first window asked for its startup files open with those. Opening a file that is already open switches to its tab, in whichever window that is (it comes to the front). Lectrix is single-instance: a second launch hands its files to the window used last.
- **Tabs:** one tab per document (or documents side by side, below), with a lock before the name of a protected document, or of one a save will protect (sections 6.8 and 6.9), which opens its properties at Security (section 6.10), a dirty marker (a dot; a spinning ring while saving) and a close prompt for unsaved changes. A tab grows from nothing to its width as it fades in when it opens, and shrinks away as it fades out when it closes, so the tabs beside it slide over (140 ms, at once with reduced motion). A document keeps its place while another tab, the Combine view or the start screen is on screen. A tab is reordered by dragging it along the row, where it follows the pointer and the others make way, or with Ctrl+Shift+Left/Right, and moves to another window when dragged off the row (section 8, Windows). Its context menu has Show beside the current tab, Show twice, side by side (Separate for a joined tab), Move to new window, Move to window (one item for each other window), Move left, Move right and Close.
- **Split view:** two to four documents side by side in one window, in columns, as one joined tab in the row (as Edge's and Chrome's split view): its documents' names side by side, a thin line between them. A document can be shown beside itself, in a second view with a place of its own ("report.pdf (2)"). A tab goes beside the one shown (beside the one shown before it, when it is on screen itself) when it is dragged off the row to the left or right edge of the pages (the outer fifth of their width, 64 to 200 px, or around a divider between panes; the column it would take shows in the accent), from its context menu (Show beside the current tab; Show twice, side by side), or with View > Side by side (Show this document twice, Show the next tab beside this one, Separate, Synchronize scrolling, Next pane, Previous pane: commands without default shortcuts). Each pane has a slim header (its document's name, the unsaved dot, and a button that gives it a tab of its own again, right after; for a second view of a document, that closes it), its banners and its pages; dragging a header sideways moves the pane among the others. Dividers between the panes resize them (dragged, or the arrows on a divider; a double-click makes them even again; 240 px at least). A press or the keyboard focus in a pane puts the focus on it: its header carries the accent (the pane itself has no ring around it), and the view bar, the side panels, the commands and the shortcuts act on it. Ctrl+W closes the pane in focus (a document shown once closes, after asking about unsaved changes); the joined tab's close button and a middle click close all its documents, each after its own question. Separate gives each document a tab of its own, in the joined tab's place.
- **Synchronized scrolling:** with documents side by side, the view bar has a lock (after the visual adjustments; checked in View > Side by side). While it is on, the pane scrolled leads and the others keep the distance they had from it: page 3 beside page 10 stays page 3 beside page 10, counted in pages, so documents of different page sizes and zooms move page for page. In single page and two pages, a page turned in one turns the others too. Panes of the same document also take the leading pane's zoom, view rotation and pages turned on screen. A pane that can go no further stops at its end, and the distances are taken again from where the panes are when another pane takes the lead.
- **Scrolling:** continuous vertical scroll, virtualized; only pages near the viewport are rendered.
- **Zoom:** fit width, fit page, preset percentages, Ctrl+wheel and pinch, centered on the cursor. Zooming glides to the new zoom in 140 ms: the + and − buttons and Ctrl+= / Ctrl+- (to the next preset, around the center of the view), the zoom menu, fit width, fit page (ending at the top of the current page) and mouse wheel notches (around the cursor). A step pressed during a glide goes on from where it was heading. A touchpad pinch follows the fingers instead, and re-fitting as the window or a pane changes size is immediate. Settings can turn gliding off, and reduced motion always does. At rest, pages are shown pixel for pixel as MuPDF rendered them: at the scale they are shown at (to the thousandth the page URL carries), one image pixel to a device pixel, at whole device pixels, so the webview never resamples them (`renderScale`, `cssPerImagePixel` in `zoom.ts`). Images of another zoom are stretched only while a zoom glides or a pinch goes on, and the low-resolution image under tiles always.
- **Navigation:** page box accepts a physical page number or a page label (typing `iv` jumps to the page labeled iv); a thumbnails panel; back/forward history for jumps (Alt+Left/Right).
- **Text:** selection and copy across lines and pages; a side of the selection (where it starts or where it ends) can be dragged to extend or reduce it, with handles drawn on the sides if Settings asks for them, and Shift with the arrow keys does the same from the keyboard; search with match highlighting and next/previous (Search, below).
- **Search** (Ctrl+F): a bar at the top right of the pages, which slides down 8 px into place as it fades in (140 ms) and goes the same way (100 ms), at once with reduced motion. It searches as you type, once typing has paused for 250 ms, without moving the view: the bar counts the matches ("52"), and Enter and Shift+Enter (F3, Shift+F3) go to the next and previous match, saying where it is ("3 of 52"). With none gone to yet, next is the first match from the current page on and previous the last one before it. Closing the bar keeps its text, which is searched again (the view left where it is) when the bar opens again. Under the field, a list suggests the words of the document that contain what is typed, as Acrobat's does: each word as the document spells it most often, with the number of matches a search for it finds, most first, at most eight, and not the word typed itself. A click, or the arrow keys and Enter, search for one and go to its first match from the current page on; Esc closes the list, then the bar. With text selected, Ctrl+F puts it in the field (on one line, at most 200 characters) and starts at the selection itself, unless the document forbids copying. While the bar is open, marks beside the pages' scrollbar show where the matches are in the document, the one shown taller and in a stronger colour; Settings can turn them off. Going to a match glides there in 140 ms, as a bookmark is glided to (Settings: "Smooth scrolling to search results", on by default; reduced motion turns it off).
- **Links:** a link in the text is followed with the Select tool (a push button of a form is a link too, with its `/A` action, else its mouse-up action `/AA /U`; other fields are not): the pointer shows the hand over it, its area is tinted meanwhile (the link's `/Rect`, in Lectrix blue at 16%), a tooltip says where it leads, and a click follows it. The tooltip is the comment tooltip of section 6.5 (the same look, place and delay): "Page 176" for a place in the document, naming the page as the page box does (its label, else its number), the address for a web or other address (cut at 160 characters), and what it is for the rest ("Another file: appendix.pdf", "JavaScript action, which Lectrix doesn’t run", "Leads nowhere in this document"). Settings can turn off the tint and the tooltip, each by itself. A place in the document (a line of a table of contents, a cross-reference) is gone to as a bookmark's is (gliding there, section 6.2), recorded in back/forward history, so Alt+Left returns. A web address (http or https) opens in the system's default browser, and a notification says which address that was ("Opened in your browser", then the address). Rust opens it (`open_link`), and only an address that a link on that page of the document carries (for a bookmark: that bookmark), so the webview can have no other page opened, and nothing but a web address is ever handed to the system. Any other address (mail, a file), another file or an action is described in a notification (an address with Copy link), never opened or run. Bookmarks are followed the same way (section 6.2). A drag that starts on a link selects its text and follows nothing, and an annotation or a side of the selected text comes before a link under it; the other tools leave links alone.
- **Scroll modes** (View > Page display, and a button in the view bar whose icon shows the mode in use): single page, single page continuous (the default), two pages and two pages continuous, plus "Cover page alone" for the two-page modes (the first page on its own, on the right, as a book's cover). A document opens in the mode Settings chooses: one of the four, or "As the document asks", its `/PageLayout` (single page continuous when it asks for none; the `…Right` layouts turn the cover on). The mode belongs to the tab and is not written to the file.
- **View rotation:** rotating the view (View > Rotate view) turns every page as it is shown and does not modify the document. One page can be turned by itself, on top of that: the Pages panel's rotate buttons turn the current page, and a thumbnail's context menu turns its page. That is on screen only too, never written to the file: nothing to save or undo, and a file open for reading only has it. The view rotation and each page's turn are remembered for the file with its view position, in app data. Pages are rotated in the document (`/Rotate`, undoable) in the page organizing tool (section 6.7), and only there.
- **Full screen** (View > Full screen, F11): the window fills the screen and shows the pages alone. The title bar, the view bar and the side panes slide out of the window (140 ms, at once with reduced motion) over the pages, which have all of it at once, and the window fills the screen as the last of them goes. Leaving is the same the other way round: they slide back in over the pages, and the window returns as they arrive. It ends with F11 again, with Esc once nothing else is left for it to step back from, or with the Leave full screen button at the end of the toolbar. Meanwhile the annotation toolbar floats, whatever Settings say of its placement, at the edge and with the visibility Settings give the floating toolbar (section 6.6), and holds the page and zoom controls before the tools. At the left or the right of the screen all of them are stacked, as the tools are there in a window (section 8): previous and next page, the page box with the position under it in small text ("of 312", or "(4 of 312)" on two lines), zoom out, the zoom level, zoom in, the page display and the visual adjustments, whose menus and panel open beside the toolbar. The pointer at the left or right side of the screen brings that side's pane, which lies over the pages (they keep their size) and goes once the pointer has left it. Alt held down shows both panes at once, and the toolbar when it shows only near its edge.
- **Visual adjustments** (a button among the page and zoom controls, after the page display, with a dot on it while anything is adjusted): a panel with Brightness and Contrast (sliders, 50% to 150% in steps of 5, 100% as the pages are), Sepia (a slider, 0% to 100% in steps of 5, 0% as the pages are: an e-book's sepia, which gives the paper the tone of old paper, the more the further, and leaves black text black), Invert colours and Greyscale (switches), and Reset. Greyscale and Invert colours change the pages and leave the annotations their colours: under greyscale they are as on the page as it is, and on an inverted page their lightness is inverted with their hue kept (black ink turns white, a yellow highlight is a gold the light text over it stays readable on). They apply at once, to the document's pages and to its thumbnails in the Pages panel, and to nothing else in the app. They are how the pages are shown, never a change to the file: nothing to save or undo, and a file open for reading only has them too. A document is shown with the adjustments Settings give every document (section 6.6: the pages as they are, until changed there), and follows them as they change. Changed in the panel, it has adjustments of its own from what it showed, remembered for its file with its view position, in app data; Reset takes them away again, and it follows Settings as before. The dot is there while the pages are not as the file has them, whichever of the two adjusts them.
- **Pages panel toolbar:** under the panel's title, rotate the current page counter-clockwise or clockwise (as it is shown, never in the document: see View rotation; the page's context menu in the viewer has no rotation, the thumbnail's does), make thumbnails smaller or larger (64 to 456 px wide, never wider than the panel), fit them to the panel's width (a toggle; they follow the width as the pane is resized, until Smaller or Larger is pressed), and switch between one column and a grid of as many columns as fit (one button whose icon shows the layout in use; arrow keys move across and down the grid). A size from these buttons glides there in 140 ms, as zooming does (off with smooth zooming, or reduced motion); resizing the pane follows it at once. The size, the fit and the layout are remembered in app data with the panes.
- **Dragging the view on a thumbnail:** a press on a thumbnail that moves more than 4 px moves the view over that page, as Acrobat's page thumbnails do: the part of the page in view is outlined on the thumbnail in the accent colour (a 2 px edge over a 12% tint) for as long as the drag lasts, and follows the pointer. Pressed inside that part, the pointer holds it where it was pressed; pressed elsewhere on the thumbnail, that part comes to the pointer first. The view moves at once (no glide), outside back/forward history, as far as it can go, and the point under the pointer stays on the page (`centerOn` and `visibleArea` on the viewer, `thumbToPage` and `pageToThumb` in `viewer/thumbs.ts`). A page turned on screen is followed as its thumbnail shows it. Let go, the outline goes and the view stays; the release does not go to the page as a click does. Meanwhile the list does not scroll to follow the current page.
- **Start screen** (no document open): the app's icon and name, then the tools as cards (Open, Combine files, Organize pages, Unlock, Protect, Compress; without the menus' "…"), then the recent files. Organize pages asks for a file, as Open… does, and shows its pages as a grid to organize (section 6.7); a file that is open already is organized in its tab. Unlock asks for a file and saves it, or a copy of it, without its password and restrictions (section 6.8); Protect asks for a file and saves it, or a copy of it, with a password, restrictions or both (section 6.9). The cards are three to a row, 124 px tall (taller where a narrow window wraps a line, ADR 0030); a last row that is not full is shared out, one card across the whole row, two half each (`toolSpans` in `starttools.ts`). Each has its icon in the accent on a small tile, the command's shortcut at the top right where it has one (an arrow otherwise, shown under the pointer), then its name and a line on what it does ("Browse for a PDF", "Merge several PDFs into one", "Reorder, rotate or delete", "Remove a password", "Add a password", "Reduce file size"). At rest a card lies on the start screen with a soft shadow; under the pointer or the keyboard focus it lifts 3 px, its border turns blue, its icon tile takes a tint of the accent and rises with a small spring, and the arrow slides in. Pressed, it sinks back a little, and a ripple runs out from where it was pressed (from its centre for Enter and Space), clipped to the card. The timings are in section 8.3; reduced motion changes the states at once, with no ripple. With documents (or the Combine view) open, the app's icon and name in the title bar show the start screen over them: nothing closes, the tabs stay with none of them selected, and the same click, a tab or Ctrl+Tab goes back (Ctrl+Tab to the document it was shown over). Opening a file from there, or a recent file that is open already, goes to its tab; a tab closed from there leaves the start screen showing, unless it has changes to ask about.
- **Recent files** list and remembered per-file view position (page and zoom), view rotation, pages turned on screen and the document's own visual adjustments, stored in app data, not in the PDF. The start screen shows the list newest first, as a table or a grid (two buttons beside the "Recent files" heading switch between them, the mark of the one shown gliding to the one picked as the active tool's does; the choice is remembered in app data with the panes). Switching brings the files in animated: each rises into place as it fades in, the first few a moment apart, 140 ms in all (at once with reduced motion); they are not animated when the start screen first shows them. The table has the name with Lectrix's PDF file icon (the folder in its tooltip), when it was last opened ("Today, 19:07", "Yesterday, 22:16", then the day and month, and the year before this one; the hour unpadded, in the system's 12- or 24-hour style) and the size as File Explorer counts it. The row under the pointer or the keyboard focus takes the tool cards' colour and edge, with rounded corners, 4 px wider than the lines between the rows on each side, and the lines above and below it go. The grid shows each file's first page, with its annotations, in a page-shaped area, and under it the name, then the date and size ("Today, 19:07 · 1.5 MB"); a file that is missing, needs a password or cannot be read shows the file icon instead. A click on a row or card opens the file; a button on it, shown on hover or focus, removes it from the list. A star, shown on hover or focus too (in the table, before the remove button; in the grid, at the preview's top left corner, remove being at its top right), pins the file: pinned files stay at the top of the list in the order they were pinned, keep their place when opened, show their star (filled, in the accent) at all times, and are never dropped however many other files are opened (the list keeps 20 besides them). Unpinned, a file goes back among the others by when it was last opened. A right-click on a row or card, or Shift+F10, offers Open, Pin to the top of the list (Unpin for a pinned file), Open file location and Remove from the list. Open file location shows the file selected in its folder in File Explorer (on macOS, Show in Finder; on Linux, Show in folder, through the file manager's FileManager1 interface where it has one, else the folder alone). Rust opens it from the recent list's path, never from a path the webview sends (`show_recent_in_folder`). A file that is gone opens its folder alone, and a folder that is gone is said so in a notification.

## Settled details

- Single page and two pages show one row at a time. Scrolling, Page Down, the down arrow or Space past its end shows the next row from its top; the same back past its start shows the previous one from its bottom. A mouse wheel notch at the edge turns at once; a touchpad turns once it has gone a notch past the edge, and once per gesture. Left and Right turn when the row is no wider than the view; Home and End go to the first and last row. Turning is not recorded in back/forward history.
- In the two-page modes, pages of a row meet at the middle of the view, a page alone keeps its side (a cover on the right, a last page on the left), and pages of different heights are centred on each other. The current page is the row's first; previous and next page turn a row; fit width and fit page fit the row's two pages. Changing the mode keeps the current page and refits a fitted zoom.
- The view bar's Previous page and Next page buttons glide to the page in 140 ms, as the view glides to an annotation picked in the list (section 6.5). Pressed again meanwhile, they go on from the page the glide was heading to; scrolling or clicking stops the glide. In single page and two pages there is nothing to glide over, so the row turned to comes in instead: it rises 24 px into place as it fades in, or comes down for the row before. Settings can turn this off ("Smooth scrolling to the next and previous page", on by default), and reduced motion always does. Rows turned by scrolling or with the keys are not animated.
- Fit width and fit page use the current page when applied, and re-fit only when the window size or the view rotation changes or a page is turned on screen, not while scrolling past pages of other sizes.
- A page turned on screen is laid out on its side or upside down, with the pixels it has (they are turned with the page, not rendered again for the turn). The view stays on the current page. Text selection, links, search hits and annotations are where the page shows them, as with a rotated view, and new annotations are placed upright as seen there (section 6.5). Its thumbnail shows it turned too; thumbnails do not follow the view rotation. A turn belongs to its page: it follows the page when pages are moved, deleted or inserted (section 6.7). The grid of pages being organized, the start screen's previews and the Combine view show pages as the file has them.
- Search is case-insensitive, starts at the current page and wraps. Search jumps are not recorded in back/forward history; page-box and thumbnail jumps are.
- A suggestion's words are found within a line (`words_containing` in `text.rs`, in the same pass as the matches): each match of the query taken out to the word boundaries on both sides (letters, digits, combining marks, an apostrophe between two of them), with spaces in the query matching a run of them, so a query of two words suggests the words around them too. They are gathered case-insensitively (`FoundWords` in `viewer/suggestions.ts`). A suggestion's count is what a search for it finds, so it counts the longer words it is part of ("lecture" counts "lectures"); a match that runs from one line onto the next is not counted, which can leave a suggestion a match or two short of its search. The list waits while typing has not yet started a search for the field's text, rather than show another query's words.
- After an edit, the search runs again and the match shown stays the one shown (the first from its page on), without moving the view (`SearchState.refresh`). Enter or a match button pressed before the search has reached the pages it needs goes there once it has.
- A search started from selected text shows first the match whose first line holds the selection's first character (`firstHit` in `doc.svelte.ts`); with none there, the first match from that page on.
- The marks stand for the whole document laid out end to end (`searchMarks` in `viewer/searchmarks.ts`), where the scrollbar shows them when all the pages scroll; in single page and two pages they still stand for the whole document. One mark per pixel row, over the scrollbar's track, which they let the pointer through to. In single page and two pages, a match in another row has that row come in, as a row turned to does.
- Copying respects the document's copy permission.
- The selected text is drawn by Lectrix, so the system would see no selection: a hidden copy of it, selected as the webview's own selection, stands in while the view has the focus (`updateMirror` in `Viewer.svelte`). The accessibility API's selected text (which translators and dictionaries such as Easydict read, and screen readers announce), Edit > Copy in macOS's menu bar and its Services menu, and Linux's primary selection then have the text Copy selection copies. A field being typed in keeps its own selection. A document that forbids copying gives the system nothing, and a selection over more than ten pages whose text hasn't been read yet is not handed over.
- A double-click selects the word under the pointer, a triple-click its line; a drag that goes on from either keeps the word or line whole and selects on from it by whole words (or whole lines): the word the pointer is in is selected whole, as in a word processor (`extendByUnit` in `viewer/selection.ts`).
- A word is a run of letters, digits and marks (accents, the vowel signs and viramas of Indic scripts, Arabic and Hebrew points), with `_`, `-` and apostrophes. In the scripts written without spaces between words (Thai, Lao, Khmer, Myanmar, Chinese, Japanese) it is the word of such a run that the system's dictionaries find (`Intl.Segmenter`, `wordSpan` in `viewer/selection.ts`); a double-click and Ctrl with the arrow keys go by these words.
- A page's text comes from MuPDF. An embedded font with no ToUnicode map has its text read off its own Unicode cmap (ADR 0029), as PDFium and Acrobat read it. Characters with no Unicode and no width are left out of the text; those with a width stay, as U+FFFD.
- The sides of a text selection are the start of its first line and the end of its last. Within 5 px of one, the pointer shows the resize arrows along the line; a drag from there moves that side while the other stays, and past the other side the selection turns over. Nothing is drawn on the sides unless Settings' "Show handles on selected text" is on: then each side has a bar the height of its line with a knob, at the top of the start and at the bottom of the end, the same size at any zoom and in one blue in both themes (the page is white in both); the handles change nothing else. A side pressed and let go changes nothing; a double-click there still selects a word, and Shift+click extends as before. A side comes before an annotation under it. With a markup tool, the changed selection is marked on release, as any selection is.
- With text selected and the page in focus, Shift and an arrow key move the end of the selection (the side last dragged or moved by the keys; after a plain drag, where it was let go) while the other side stays: Left and Right by a character, Up and Down by a line (keeping its place across shorter lines), and with Ctrl too, Right to the end of the word and Left to the start of one, Down to the end of the paragraph and Up to its start, then the next one's. Left and Right follow the text's reading order. The keys cross lines and pages (pages without text are passed), never leave nothing selected (a step that would end on the other side goes on past it, and the selection turns over), and scroll just far enough to keep the moving end in view. Without a selection or a text cursor the keys scroll as before; a selection is started from the keyboard only from the text cursor.
- A click in the text with the Select tool or a markup tool puts a text cursor there (`DocTab.caret`): a 1.5 px black line (`--lectrix-text-caret`; the page is white in both themes) as high as the character beside it, blinking as Windows' cursor does (530 ms on, 530 off; steady with reduced motion) and shown only while the pages have the keyboard focus. Left and Right move it by a character, Up and Down by a line, with Ctrl by a word and a paragraph, as Shift with them moves the end of a selection, except that the end of a line and the start of the next are two places for it; meanwhile they don't scroll or turn rows (Page Up, Page Down, Home and End still do). Shift with them selects from it, and the selection then goes on as one made with the pointer: with the Select tool the quick tools come, with a markup tool it is marked (section 6.5). A click that follows a link or selects an annotation puts no cursor; a click elsewhere, Esc, the other tools (pen, note, text box), a selection with the pointer or any change to the document takes it away. Switching between Select and a markup tool keeps it.
- Full screen comes and goes in two steps (`Stage` in `fullscreen/fullscreen.svelte.ts`): the slide, then the window. In that order because switching the window makes the webview lay out and draw everything again at the new size, which holds its frames up for 40 to 100 ms (most the first time): a slide started with the switch was lost in that, and one that waits for it starts 150 ms after the key. During the slide the bars and panes are laid out as in a window, and the pages have the whole window or screen under them (`viewer-covers`), so no gap opens where a bar was. They move by transforms and opacity alone, which the compositor does without laying anything out. The window is asked to switch just before the slide ends (50 ms going in, 20 ms coming back, the time it takes to answer), and in the frame that brings its new size everything is laid out for it, once. The toolbar is full screen's own from the start of the slide in to the start of the slide out, and the notification that says how to leave goes when leaving.
- Split view (`SplitView.svelte`, `features/split/`): a document is a `DocModel` shared by its views (`DocTab`), which keep their own zoom, place, rotation, page display, selection, search and history; a change made in one view reaches every view of the document. A joined tab is a `TabGroup` of up to four views (`stores/group.svelte.ts`). The pane that joins gets the focus. While a tab is dragged, the tab on screen stays on screen; a tab is shown when it is let go (a click, the end of a drag along the row, a drop), not as it is pressed. Dragged to an edge of the pages, a tab goes beside the tab on screen, or, when it is that tab itself, beside the tab shown before it (`shownBefore`), so a tab goes beside itself only when it is the window's only tab; the column it would take shows over the pages on screen. Dropped over the middle of the pages, or anywhere else in its window, it stays and is shown (section 8); Esc leaves everything as it was. A joined tab moved to another window (its menu, File > Move tab to new window, a drag) moves all its documents, which arrive there as tabs of their own; a second view of a document stays behind and goes. While the pane in focus is organized (section 6.7), its grid is in its column and the side panes go, as for a tab. Full screen keeps the panes, without their headers and banners. The lock is not remembered; a tab of one pane, and a new arrangement of panes, scroll by themselves until it is turned on again.
- Synchronized scrolling (`features/split/sync.ts`): a pane's place is its page and how far down that page the top of the view is (`place()`); a pane following another goes there at once (`follow()`), outside back/forward history, and its own scroll leads nothing. The lead is whichever pane scrolls by other means: the wheel, the scrollbar, the keys, a page turned with the view bar, a bookmark or link followed.
- Full screen starts only with a document on screen, and ends by itself when none is (the last tab closed, the Combine view shown); switching tabs keeps it. It belongs to its window and is not remembered: a window closed full screen comes back at the place it had before. The first time it starts while Lectrix runs, a notification says how to leave it and how to reach the panes. A file open for reading only shows no PDF/A banner meanwhile (a change tried there still says why not, with Enable editing); the banner of a file changed on disk shows.
- Full screen, the pointer brings a pane once it has rested for 150 ms within 4 px of that side of the screen with no button down, so a drag never brings one and the pages' scrollbar beside it stays usable. The pane goes 300 ms after the pointer has left it, but not during a drag (its edge being resized), while the pointer is on a menu, or while text is typed in the pane. A pane a command asks for (the panel and pane commands, Ctrl+B) shows until it is hidden again or the page is pressed. The pane's button, in its row, hides it, and the pointer must then leave that side before it brings the pane again. The panes the window had open are kept for when full screen ends, and nothing done meanwhile changes them.
- Alt counts as held after 150 ms by itself: with another key it is that shortcut's Alt (Alt+Left) and shows nothing. Pressing the page ends what Alt shows (an area highlight is drawn with Alt held), and letting Alt go keeps the pane the pointer is on. Full screen, Alt by itself does not reach the system, which would open the window's menu. A toolbar that shows only near its edge also shows for 1.5 s as full screen starts.
- Visual adjustments are a CSS filter on each page's layer (`pageFilter` in `viewer/adjustments.ts`): its pixels and what is drawn over them (the selection, search hits, a text box being typed), so what shows on a page stays as visible as it was. The page's shadow, and the bars and panels over the pages, are outside it. Greyscale comes first, then the inversion, then brightness and contrast, which so act on what is seen (less brightness dims an inverted page's light text). Sepia is not in the filter: CSS's `sepia()` leaves white paper nearly white and takes the colours away. It is the tone of old paper (`--lectrix-sepia-paper`, `#F0E0BD`) laid over the page and multiplied into it (`.page-tone`, `pageTone`), as much of it as the slider says: white paper takes the tone, black stays black, pictures keep their colours, warmed. It lies over the filtered page, so it tones what is seen: an inverted page keeps its dark paper and has its light text warmed, and more brightness does not wash the tone out. A document's own adjustments are `DocTab.ownAdjustments` (null while it follows Settings) and `ViewState.own_adjustments`; a view stored up to 1.1, which had adjustments whether anything was adjusted or not, gives the document its own only if something was. A change that leaves a document looking as Settings say takes its own away. After Reset the keyboard focus goes to the first slider, since the button is disabled then. Invert colours inverts lightness and keeps hues (`invert(1) hue-rotate(180deg)`), roughly: light colours come out dark and muddy. An inverted page waits dark for its pixels.
- The annotations are out of the colours part of the filter (greyscale and the inversion, `colourFilter`); brightness and contrast (`lightFilter`) are on the page's layer, annotations included. While the colours are adjusted, a page that shows annotations (`DocTab.annotationLayerOn`; the annotation list's hidden ones do not count) is two images from Rust (`layer` in the page URL, `protocol.rs`): its content without annotations (`layer=content`, form fields included), filtered, and over it its annotations alone (`render::annotation_layer`), unfiltered. That layer is the content with the page's highlights and then its other annotations drawn over it, with every pixel they did not change made transparent, so under greyscale it shows exactly what the page as it is shows there, what lies under a highlight included. For an inverted page (`layer=annots-dark`) each of its pixels has its HSL lightness inverted with hue and saturation kept, and it is laid over the page with `mix-blend-mode: screen`, so the light text under a highlight stays light. Pixels of highlights (`dark_tint`) are kept between 0.2 and 0.4 luminance when saturated (a full yellow is dimmed under white text, a pale pink at 40% brightened), pixels of other annotations (`dark_stroke`) only lit to 0.2 when saturated (pure blue). The content is cached as any image and shared with the layer, which Rust draws over it; pages without annotations are rendered as they are. Thumbnails do the same, and rendering ahead asks for the layer, which renders the content with it. Selection, search hits, the link under the pointer, what is being drawn and the text box being typed get only the inversion of the colours filter (`overlayFilter`): greyscale leaves them their colours, as it leaves the annotations theirs. The panel opens with a click or Enter; Esc closes it and nothing else. The start screen's previews and the Combine view show pages as they are.
