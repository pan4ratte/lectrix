# 6.5 Annotations (user interface)

Part of the Lectrix specification. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and [README.md](README.md) lists these files; sections keep the numbers they share, so "section 5.4" is in AGENTS.md and "section 6.5" in [annotations.md](annotations.md).

- **Toolbar tools:** Select, Highlight, Underline, Strikeout, Squiggly, Note, Pen, Text box.
- **Text markup:** with a markup tool active, selecting text creates the annotation on mouse-up. A click in the text puts a blinking text cursor there (section 6.1); Shift with the arrow keys selects from it, and letting Shift go creates the annotation. Alt-drag creates an area highlight (one rectangular quad) for scanned pages without text. New text markup (from a tool, the quick tools or Alt-drag) is not selected, unless Settings' "Add a comment after marking text" is on: then it is selected and the cursor goes in its note, in the inspector, or in its row of the annotation list while the list shows it. Undo (Ctrl+Z) in that comment while it is empty, before anything else has changed the document, takes the new annotation back (`undoMadeForComment` in `annotations/actions.ts`); text typed there is the field's to undo first. A new note's comment, which opens the same way, does the same.
- **Quick tools:** with the Select tool, selected text gets a floating bar above the point where the pointer was released (below it when there is no room above; released on the last selected line, the bar keeps clear of that line), with the colour button first (the colour every tool draws with, so it can be picked before the text is marked; its panel has no opacity, which is each tool's own), then the tools chosen in Settings: Highlight, Underline, Strikeout, Squiggly, Highlight with note, Copy, Add bookmark. Picking a colour there leaves the selection and the bar.
- **Select tool on annotations:** a click on an annotation selects it as an object. A drag that starts on one that doesn't move (text markup, and shapes, stamps and others Lectrix can't move) and over text selects that text instead, as a drag anywhere else does; notes, drawings and text boxes are moved by a drag.
- **Annotation bar:** clicking an annotation without a comment selects it and shows a floating bar above it: color, type (text markup only: highlight, underline, strikeout, squiggly), note, delete. Clicking an annotation with a comment (not a text box, whose text is on the page) shows its inspector instead, with the bar's controls in its header. Double-clicking puts the cursor in the inspector's note; while the annotation list shows the annotation, at the end of its comment in the list instead.
- **Style:** one color for every tool, which new annotations get whatever the tool, and an opacity, a stroke width (pen) and a font size (text box) for each tool. The toolbar's color button is always there, before the tools, so a color can be picked before a tool is; its panel has the opacity of the active tool, and none with the Select tool. The number keys 1 to 9 pick the first nine presets as that color (commands with shortcuts, Settings > Shortcuts), and never recolor a selected annotation. 18 preset colors (Acrobat's until changed in Settings, section 6.6, and the same for every tool; three rows of six, 6 px apart: bright colors, yellow, green, red and blue first, then orange and purple, their light tints under them, white to black; arrow keys move among them) plus a custom color and opacity (a slider, 10% to 100%), in one panel that a button showing the color in use opens, as Acrobat's picker does; the same in the toolbar, the annotation bar, the inspector and the annotation list's selected row. The panel opens when the pointer has rested on the button for 150 ms (Settings can turn this off), and on a click or Enter. Opened by the pointer, it takes no focus and goes 300 ms after the pointer has left the button and the panel; once something in it is pressed or focused, or the button clicked, it stays until Esc or a click elsewhere, as a panel opened by a click does. Under the presets is the opacity slider with its value and, at the end of that row, the custom color's button, which slides Lectrix's own picker open below (140 ms, at once with reduced motion; open from the start when the color in use is not a preset): a saturation and brightness square, a hue bar, the eyedropper, and a field that takes a hex value only (six or three digits, no RGB or HSL). A color applies when picked (from the picker: when the pointer is let go, on Enter or leaving the field, or half a second after the arrow keys stop) and the panel stays open for the opacity, which applies when the slider is let go, stroke width for the pen; the color and each tool's last-used style are remembered, including a color given to an existing annotation, which new annotations then get, and an opacity, which new ones of its type then get (Settings can turn this off: new annotations then start from the defaults each time Lectrix starts). Until changed, the color is Acrobat's highlight yellow (`#FFD100`, the first preset), highlights have Acrobat's 40% opacity and the other tools draw opaque. Versions up to 1.2 kept a color for each tool: the highlight's became the one color, and presets changed in Settings kept their colors in the new order.
- **Inspector panel** (the comment panel) for the selected annotation: a header with the bar's controls (color, stroke width or font size, type, Properties where the bar has it, and delete at the right end), then note text, replies and repair. It sits beside the annotation (right, else left, else below), scrolls with the page, and a tail with curved sides points at the annotation's centre (its sides leave the panel's border along it, as the border's continuation, and no border runs across its base): from the side facing it, opposite that centre, or out of a corner when the centre is off that corner. It drags from anywhere but its controls, within the visible part of the view, and resizes from every edge and corner. Where it was put and the size it was given are kept for each annotation while the app runs. Other annotations' inspectors take the last size given as a limit: its width, and no more height than their content needs. While the annotation list is on screen and has a row for the selected annotation, the page shows neither its inspector nor its bar: the row stands in for both, with their controls above the comment. A reply shown in a thread has no row, so it gets its inspector or its bar.
- **Properties dialog** for the selected annotation, from Properties in the page's context menu (or the bar of an annotation Lectrix can't change): the author, changed with Save (one undo step), and the creation and modification dates.
- **Annotation list** in the right pane by default (section 8): click to jump, edit note text, delete, "needs repair" badges. Its header shows how many annotations the document has ("5 of 12 annotations" while some are hidden) and three buttons. **Search** opens a field (sliding open, as the filters do) that filters the rows as you type (once typing has paused for 200 ms, so the rows and the pages are not worked out again for every key; an emptied field applies at once), by their comments and replies, or, with the switch in the field, by the text they mark (text markup only; each page's text is read the first time). Esc empties the field, then closes it. **Sort** orders the rows by page (the default), author, date created or date modified, A–Z (first page, A, oldest first; the default) or Z–A, the order kept when the sort changes; rows without an author or a date go last either way. Settings keep the sort last picked (on by default, section 6.6): every document's list is sorted that way, in every window and after a restart. With that off, each document starts sorted by page, A–Z, and keeps its own sort while it is open. The rows of a page go as they appear on it, not as they were made: top to bottom, and left to right among those that start on the same line (text markup starts where its text does). They show under headings of the page, the author or the day. **Filter** opens a panel below the header with the types present as their icons, the colours present as swatches and the authors as pills, each under its subheading (Type, Colour, Author); picking several in one row shows any of them, and the rows combine. A dot on the button marks picked filters; Clear filters empties them. Each row shows the type as an icon in the annotation's colour, moved toward the text colour only as far as 3:1 contrast on the pane needs (its name as tooltip and accessible name), then the author and a short date, then the whole comment with its line breaks. Each page's group starts with a header under a dividing line. A right-click (or Shift+F10) offers Reply, Copy comment and Delete annotation; on a reply in a thread, Edit reply, Copy comment and Delete reply.
- An empty annotation list says so (section 8.2): "No annotations" and "Highlight text [H] or add a note [N] from the toolbar.", the keys as Settings has them; a document that can't be annotated says why instead ("This document is open for reading only.", "This document’s security settings don’t allow adding annotations.", with an **Unlock…** button, section 6.8). In such a document the toolbar's tools other than Select look unavailable, and a click on one, or its shortcut, says why in a notification instead of picking it. When the search or the filters leave out every annotation, it says "No matches" and which of them does ("No annotations match the search and the filters."), with a button that clears them (Clear search, Clear filters, or Clear search and filters) and puts the focus on the list.
- Annotations can be moved, resized (ink, text box, notes) and deleted; every change is undoable.
- Author name is set in Settings > Annotations, defaulting to the user name of the system account.

## Settled details

- With the Select tool, a press on an annotation that doesn't move, over text, waits: let go within 3 px of where it was pressed, it is a click and selects the annotation; moved further, the annotation is not selected and the text is, from the press (the `press` gesture in `Viewer.svelte`). A press away from text, a double-click, Shift and the corner handles act at once, as before.
- With a markup tool, text selected from the text cursor (section 6.1) is marked when Shift is let go, as a drag is when the pointer is: with that tool, in its last-used style. The cursor then stays at the end the keys moved, so they can go on (unless the new markup opened its comment, Settings). A selection made by the keys with the Select tool is not marked by picking a markup tool afterwards.
- The quick tools' markup uses each tool's last-used style; "Highlight with note" makes a highlight and puts the cursor in its note (in the inspector, or in the annotation list while that shows it); "Add bookmark" does what Ctrl+B does. Tools the document forbids are shown disabled; with none allowed or none chosen, no bar appears. The default set is Highlight, Underline, Strikeout, Highlight with note and Copy.
- Each selected annotation shows either its inspector or its bar: the inspector if it had a comment when selected (it stays while the comment is edited, even emptied), or when opened from the bar, a double-click or the annotation list; otherwise the bar. Selecting another annotation decides again for that one. The inspector closes with Esc (from a control other than the note), and the bar takes its place until the annotation is clicked again, or when nothing is selected. While the annotation list shows the annotation, its row has both, and the page neither.
- Changing an annotation's type keeps its quads, color, opacity, note and author (one undo step, "Change highlight to underline"). The bar of a text box edits its text in place, as a double-click does; an annotation Lectrix can't change gets a Properties button instead.
- Notes are placed with one click; the inspector opens focused on the note field (while the annotation list shows the note, the cursor goes in its row's comment instead). Text boxes are typed in place; Ctrl+Enter or a click outside finishes, Esc discards. An empty new box is not created; emptying an existing one deletes it (one undo step).
- The inspector's color for a text box is its text color (rule 6 in section 5.1).
- Deleting an annotation that has replies asks first, then deletes the replies and the popup with it.
- A text box from another app that has a callout line can be edited but not moved or resized.
- The list shows markup, notes, drawings, text boxes, shapes, stamps and attachments; popups, links and form widgets are not listed.
- Reply opens a field under the thread; Ctrl+Enter or leaving it sends, Esc drops it, and an empty reply is not written. Edit reply edits in place the same way; emptying a reply deletes it. Replies are written only to annotations in the list's rows, not to replies, as Acrobat's list does; each is one undo step ("Add reply", "Edit reply", "Delete reply").
- While the list is on screen, the annotations its search or filters leave out are hidden on the page too: not drawn there (the page image is asked for without them), and not found by the pointer. Replies go with their thread. Thumbnails keep showing every annotation, and so does the page once the list is closed or another panel shows in its place. Nothing vanishes under the user's hands: the selected annotation keeps its row and stays on the page though a change (its colour, its type, its comment) made it stop matching, until it is deselected; another search or other filters deselect an annotation they leave out; and an annotation that has just appeared (made, or brought back by undo) clears a search and filters that would leave it out.
- The list's date is the last change, as Acrobat shortens it: the time for today, day and month this year, with the year before that; the full date and time are in its tooltip and in the Properties dialog.
- The list's order on a page is by position alone: annotations start on the same line when their tops are no further apart than half the height of the shorter one (for text markup, its first line). Text in columns is not followed: a mark at the top of the second column comes before one lower in the first. Z–A by page shows the last page first, each page still from its top. Rows that another sort can't tell apart (one author, the same time) go in page order, then by position.
- A selected row stands in for the annotation's inspector and bar, which the page doesn't show meanwhile. Above its comment it has the inspector's controls (color, stroke width or font size, type, Properties where the inspector has it, and delete at the right end), and it edits its comment in place in a field as tall as the text, at the same text size. The controls slide open as the row is selected (140 ms, at once with reduced motion), with the field when there is no comment yet, while the last selected row's slide shut. A row selected on screen keeps its comment where it was meanwhile: the list scrolls with the controls as far as it can, so they rise out from under the comment, and the comment is brought into view afterwards if it isn't; a row has the same room on every side, so the controls and the field sit evenly in it. It comes into view when the annotation is selected on the page. What would put the cursor in the inspector's note puts it at the end of the row's comment: a double-click on the annotation, a new note, new text markup that opens its comment, "Highlight with note". Up, Down and Delete pressed on the row's controls are theirs: they neither move the selection nor delete the annotation. A comment shown as text is inset as the field that edits it, so it stays where it is when its row is selected, and the row's first line (type, author, date) starts at the same inset.
- A comment typed in the inspector's note or in the selected row's field is written when the field is left, however that happens: a click anywhere (on the page too, which deselects the annotation), Tab, Ctrl+Enter or Esc, which both leave the field and keep the text. Nothing discards it; undo takes it back (one step).
- Picking an annotation in the list scrolls the page to it with a 140 ms glide (Settings: "Smooth scrolling to annotations", on by default; reduced motion turns it off). A long way starts two screens from the annotation. Scrolling or clicking stops the glide.
- Resting the pointer on an annotation shows its comment in a tooltip, text only with its line breaks, at most 12 lines. It follows the pointer, below it, with its tail pointing up at the pointer from just under the cursor's arrow; it shifts left, or goes above the pointer (its tail then pointing down), to stay wholly inside the page area. The delay is a Settings slider (0 to 2 s, 0.3 s by default). Text boxes show none, since their text is on the page.
- Comments' line breaks (CR, as Acrobat writes them, CR LF, LF, U+2028, U+2029) all show as breaks. A comment is written back only when its text was edited, so looking at one in a field changes nothing.
- Repair counts `/Rect` as too small only past the 1 pt margin. Unreadable QuadPoints are reported and left alone.
- Annotating a turned page places notes, drawings, text boxes and area highlights upright as seen on screen (text boxes carry `/Rotate`, as Acrobat writes); text markup follows the text. That holds for a page the document rotates and for one turned on screen only (the view rotation, a page's own turn: section 6.1): a text box typed there is typed upright as the page is shown, grows downwards there, and carries the page's `/Rotate` plus the turn (`Body::FreeText.turn`), as Acrobat writes for a box typed in a rotated view; an area highlight's corners are ordered as seen there; a note's icon goes below and right of the click as seen there. A text box is edited as its text stands: the editor is turned with it, and it is fitted to its text by its own `/Rotate`, whatever the page's is. A note's icon is drawn with the page, so on a page turned on screen only it lies on its side in Lectrix, as PDFium and pdf.js show it on a rotated page; Acrobat keeps it upright.
