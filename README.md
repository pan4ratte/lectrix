## Why Lectrix

- **Free, and private.** Open-source, with no paid version or paid features, no account and no data collected. Lectrix works offline and goes online only to check for a new version, which **Settings** can turn off.
- **The same app on Windows, macOS and Linux.** One interface and the same tools on all three systems; none of them is treated as second-class.
- **Lightweight and fast.** The Windows installer is under 9 MB. On a mid-range laptop the window is up in a tenth of a second, and the first page of a 1,000-page book is on screen within 0.6 seconds of launch.
- **Designed with care.** A modern, well thought-out interface that takes the pain out of the part of a PDF app you meet most: everyday use. Choose a light or a dark theme, dock the annotation toolbar or let it float at any edge of the pages, pick the quick tools offered for selected text, move the side panels between the two panes, and change any keyboard shortcut.
- **Careful with your files.** Saving never leaves a half-written file behind, and a copy of your unsaved changes, kept every two minutes, is offered back after a crash.


## Features

### Read comfortably

Open documents in tabs, and drag a tab away from the others to give it a window of its own. Pages scroll continuously or turn one at a time, alone or two side by side, and **View > Full screen** leaves them alone on the screen. Search suggests the document's words as you type and marks every match beside the scrollbar. Right-click a word or a passage to translate it with Google Translate or DeepL, or to look it up on Google, DuckDuckGo, Wikipedia, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar or JSTOR. **Visual adjustments** changes the brightness, contrast and sepia tone of the pages, or inverts their colours for reading at night, without touching the file.

### Documents side by side

Drag a tab to the edge of the pages to read up to four documents side by side in one tab, or show one document twice to read two parts of it at once. They can scroll and zoom together, keeping the same pages beside each other.

### Present from a PDF

**View > Presentation** (F5) shows one page at a time, as large as the screen allows, with the transitions and timings a deck exported from PowerPoint or Beamer comes with. With a projector or a second screen, the audience sees the pages and you see the presenter view: the next page, the page's notes and a timer.

### Make all kinds of annotations

Highlight, underline, strike out or squiggle text, leave a note, draw with the pen, add lines, arrows, rectangles and ellipses, or type in a text box. Select text and a small bar offers the tools you use most, with no need to pick one first. Every tool has 18 preset colours, a colour of your own and an opacity.

### Keep track of every comment

The annotation list shows every comment in a document. Search, sort and filter them by type, colour or author, and the pages show only what the list shows. Reply to a comment or edit it right in the list, or select several and change or delete them together.

### Organise a document with bookmarks

Press **Ctrl+B** to add a bookmark where you are; selected text becomes its title. Rename bookmarks, drag them into order, nest them, give them colours, and point one at another place.

### Number pages as the book does

Give the front matter roman numerals, start the body at 1, add a prefix to the appendices. The page box then understands the book's own numbers: type `iv` to go to page iv.

### Organise pages

**Document > Organize pages** shows the pages as a grid. Drag them into another order, rotate or delete them, extract them into a new document, or drop in pages from other files. Links, bookmarks and page labels stay with the pages they belong to, and every change can be undone.

### Combine files

Add several PDFs, see all their pages together in one grid, then reorder, rotate or remove pages before combining them into a new file. Bookmarks and page numbering come along.

### Compare two versions

**Compare** finds what changed between two versions of a document, word by word, and marks it on both, side by side: inserted, deleted and changed text each in its own colour. Pages without text, such as scans, are compared as pictures. Save either document with the differences marked, or a report listing them.

### Compress, protect and unlock

**Compress** makes a file smaller, without losing a pixel or by recompressing its pictures, and shows a page before and after before anything is saved. **Protect** gives a file a password, limits on printing, changing and copying, or both, with AES-256 encryption. **Unlock** removes them.

### Print and export

Print with or without annotations, choosing pages by their number or their label, with a preview of every sheet. Save pages as PNG, JPEG or TIFF images, copy a page or any part of it to paste elsewhere, or save the pictures inside a document as files, JPEG pictures without losing any quality.

### Annotations that work everywhere

What you write in Lectrix opens as you made it in Acrobat, in Edge, Chrome and Firefox, and in Foxit. **Document > Repair annotations** fixes annotations made in other apps that show wrongly, or not at all, elsewhere.

## Download and install

Get the latest version from the [releases page](https://github.com/pan4ratte/lectrix/releases/latest), and pick the file for your system:

| System | File | Requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Windows | `Lectrix_…_x64-setup.exe` | Windows 10, version 1809 or later, or Windows 11; 64-bit |
| macOS, Apple Silicon | `Lectrix_…_aarch64.dmg` | macOS 26 |
| macOS, Intel | `Lectrix_…_x64.dmg` | macOS 15 or later |
| Debian, Ubuntu and their relatives | `Lectrix_…_amd64.deb` | Debian 13, Ubuntu 24.04, or later |
| Fedora and its relatives | `Lectrix-….x86_64.rpm` | Fedora 40 or later |
| Any other Linux | `Lectrix_…_amd64.AppImage` | glibc 2.39 or later; 64-bit |

The builds are not signed with a Microsoft or Apple certificate yet, so the first install asks for one more step:

* **Windows:** if SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC", choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
* **macOS:** the first launch is blocked. Allow it in **System Settings > Privacy & Security** with **Open Anyway**.

After that Lectrix updates itself: it tells you when a new version is out and shows what it brings before you install it. [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md) lists the changes in every version.

Lectrix is developed on Windows first. The macOS and Linux builds are newer and less tested. On macOS, the versions above are the ones each build is made on: the Apple Silicon build has its automatic tests run there, the Intel build has none yet, and older versions of macOS are untried.
