# 6.7 Organize pages

Part of the Lectrix specification. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and [README.md](README.md) lists these files; sections keep the numbers they share, so "section 5.4" is in AGENTS.md and "section 6.5" in [annotations.md](annotations.md).

A document's pages as a grid, to put them in another order, rotate them, delete them, add pages from another file and copy pages into a new one. Each change is made in the document at once (ADR 0018).

- **A mode of the document's tab.** **Document > Organize pages** (a command without a default shortcut) shows the pages as a grid in the viewer's place, with a bar of its own in the view bar's place. The view bar has no button for it: the menu has the command, and the start screen the tool (section 6.1). The tabs and the banners stay. The side panes, which are for reading, are not shown meanwhile, nor are their buttons; they come back as they were. **Done** in the bar, Esc once nothing is selected, or the command again (its menu item reads **Finish organizing pages** meanwhile) go back to reading where the document was; Enter or a double-click on a page goes back to reading at that page.
- **The grid** is the Combine view's (`PageGrid.svelte`, section 6.4): a click selects a page, Ctrl+click adds or removes one, Shift+click extends, a rectangle drawn from anywhere but a picture selects what it touches, arrow keys move the focus (Shift extends) and Ctrl+A selects every page. Under each page is its label, with its place in the document after it where that says something else ("iv (4)"; "(4)" for a page whose label is empty). Under the pointer, a page's label gives way to a bar in its place, centered under the picture, as in the Combine view: the same label, then buttons that rotate that page counter-clockwise and clockwise and delete it.
- **The bar**, centered as the view bar is: rotate counter-clockwise and clockwise, delete; then **Insert**, **Extract** and **Labels**, each with its name beside its icon (pages from a file, the selected pages into a new document, the Page labels dialog); smaller and larger pictures (steps from a 96 px square, each about a third larger than the one before, up to the size at which one page fills the view; 160 px at first); how many pages there are, or are selected ("3 of 212 pages selected"); and at the end **Done**.
- **The size of the pictures** also changes with Ctrl+= and Ctrl+- (the commands that zoom the pages while reading; a step of the sizes, as the buttons), with Ctrl and the mouse wheel (a notch is about a fifth larger or smaller) and with a touchpad pinch, which follows the fingers to any size between the smallest and the largest. A size from a button, a key or a wheel notch glides there in 140 ms, as zooming the pages does (section 6.1; at once when Settings' smooth zooming is off, or with reduced motion). The page under the pointer keeps its place for the wheel and a pinch, the page in the middle of the view for the buttons and the keys. The largest size is the one at which a single page, with its label under it, fills the grid's view, so it follows the window: there the pages come one under another, as while reading, and pictures larger than a window made smaller allows take its largest size at once.
- **Move:** drag the selected pages by a picture to a gap between pages, or Alt+Shift with an arrow key for one place at a time (pages apart come together). Links and bookmarks keep leading to their pages. Page labels belong to the places: a moved page takes the label of its new place, and the label ranges stay as they are.
- **Rotate:** the bar's buttons and R and Shift+R turn the selected pages in the document (`/Rotate`); the buttons under the page the pointer is on turn that page alone.
- **Delete:** the bar's button, Del or Backspace, or the button under the page the pointer is on. Lectrix asks first ("Delete page iv?" naming the page as the page box does, or "Delete 3 pages?"; **Delete**, in the destructive colour, or **Cancel**, which Esc means). With the pages go the links and bookmarks that lead to them (a bookmark with bookmarks inside stays as a heading), the named destinations that lead to them and the form fields on them; label ranges after them start that many pages earlier, so the numbering closes up, and a range left without pages goes. At least one page stays. A notification then says how many pages were deleted, what went with them, and that the pages stay inside the file, where they can be recovered, until it is saved with Save As (optimized), with a button that does that: an ordinary save adds to the file and keeps what was there (section 5.4).
- **Insert pages from files** (section 6.4): the bar's button, or the command, asks for one file or several, then for the options, and puts their pages after the last selected page (after the page with the keyboard focus when none is selected). The menu puts them before or after the selected pages. PDFs dragged in from another program show the gap under them, as dragged pages do, with a note at the top of the grid ("Drop a PDF between two pages to insert its pages there"); dropped, their options are asked and their pages go into that gap. The inserted pages are then selected, ready to be dragged elsewhere.
- **Extract:** the bar's button, or the menu, copies the selected pages into a new document, in the order they have in the document. Nothing is asked first: Lectrix shows progress with Stop, then opens the new document in a tab of its own, with its pages as a grid (at the size this grid has), so they can be put in order at once. It is named "Pages from report.pdf" and is no file yet (section 7, documents without a file): its tab is marked unsaved, and Save asks where to save it, starting next to the document. The document keeps the pages and is not changed, so this works in a document open for reading only. The pages go as the document has them now, unsaved changes included, with their annotations, the links among them, the bookmarks that lead to them (flat) and their page labels; links and bookmarks that lead to pages left behind are not copied, and a notification says how many.
- **Page labels** (section 6.3) are set in a dialog over the grid, where each page shows its label: the bar's **Labels** button or the grid's menu opens it, for the selected pages. Nothing offers it while reading.
- **Menu:** a right click on a page, or Shift+F10 with the keyboard focus on it, offers what can be done with the selected pages (a page that is not selected becomes the selection first): Rotate clockwise, Rotate counter-clockwise, Delete page… ("Delete 3 pages…"), Extract page into a new document, Insert pages from file before…, Insert pages from file after…, Page labels…, and Read from this page.
- **Undo and redo** are the document's (section 7): each move, rotation, deletion and insertion is one step ("Move pages", "Rotate page", "Delete pages", "Insert pages"), in the grid and while reading alike.

## Settled details

- Starting the mode leaves full screen, picks the Select tool, closes the search and deselects text and annotations. The grid opens with the current page in the middle of the view and the keyboard focus on it. Meanwhile the commands that are for reading do nothing, and their menu items are disabled: Find, the annotation tools, Full screen, the panes and their panels, fitting, the page display, rotating the view, Go to page, Back and Forward and Add bookmark.
- The mode belongs to its tab, stays while another tab is shown, and is not remembered: a document opens for reading.
- What is kept by page follows its page through every such change, an undo or a redo included (`DocumentChange.pageMap`, `PageMove` in `src/lib/stores/pagemap.ts`): the viewer stays on the page it showed (on the page that took its place if it was deleted), the history of jumps follows its pages and forgets the places on deleted ones, and a selected annotation stays selected.
- A page keeps its cell in the grid, and the picture in it, while the pages around it change: the picture is drawn again for the new revision under the old one.
- The pictures show the pages as the file has them, without the document's visual adjustments, as the Combine view does.
- After a deletion, the page that followed the deleted ones is selected.
- When the Insert command showed the grid itself, giving the insertion up (no file chosen, its password not given, Cancel in the dialog) goes back to reading. The Pages panel's "Insert pages from file after this page…" does the same with the gap after that page.
- Of several files, those that cannot be used are left out and the rest go in: one that fails to open or forbids copying its content says so in a notification, and one whose password is not given is skipped; the options are asked once every password prompt is answered. If a file fails to go in, the ones before it stay and the notification counts them. Files dropped while an insertion is under way are not taken. Files dropped on the window while the grid shows are never opened in tabs.
- A document that forbids copying its content can't have pages extracted, and says so. Stopping an extraction leaves nothing behind. Pages can be extracted from a document that is itself not saved yet.
- An Esc that closes a menu or a dialog does only that: it neither deselects nor leaves the grid.
- Edits run one after another, each working out its pages when its turn comes, so a key held down cannot overtake itself.
- The pictures are rendered for the size a glide ends at, or a pinch rests at (150 ms after its last move): once, not for every frame on the way, during which the pictures they have are stretched.
- A document open for reading only (section 5.4), or whose permissions forbid assembling it, shows its grid, where pages can be selected and nothing changes them: the PDF/A banner says why, or the bar does ("This document’s security settings don’t allow changing its pages."). A signed document gets the usual warning before the first change.
