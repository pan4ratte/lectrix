# 6.6 Settings, About and installers

Part of the Lectrix specification. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) holds the rules every change follows and [README.md](README.md) lists these files; sections keep the numbers they share, so "section 5.4" is in AGENTS.md and "section 6.5" in [annotations.md](annotations.md).

- **Settings** (File menu, Ctrl+,): groups as tabs with icons down the left side (Up and Down move between them; the last one shown opens again while Lectrix runs; the fill of the one shown glides to the tab picked, and its settings rise a little as they fade in, both in 140 ms, at once with reduced motion), the chosen group's settings at the right, one per row (descriptions that name the system, such as the theme's "System follows Windows.", name the one Lectrix runs on): the name and a description, then the control, a switch for anything that is on or off, a dropdown for a choice, and a pill of segments (a radio group; arrow keys move and choose; the raised mark of the one chosen glides to it in 140 ms) for a choice between a few peers. **General:** under Opening files, whether files that claim PDF/A open for reading only (on by default, section 5.4); under Updates, whether Lectrix checks for updates automatically, once a day (on by default), and **Check now**, which closes Settings (the update notice is under the dialog, so its buttons could not be used) and asks as Help > Check for updates… does; under Reset, **Restore defaults…**, a button in the destructive colour (the red of errors, as is the one that confirms), which asks first ("Restore default settings?": Restore defaults or Cancel) and then puts every setting back to its default at once, the shortcuts and the author name included (recent files, panes and the window's place are not settings and stay). **Viewing:** under Pages, the page display documents open in (section 6.1: single page continuous by default, any scroll mode, or as the document asks); under Selected text, whether selected text shows handles on its sides (off by default, section 6.1); under Search, whether the matches of a search are marked beside the scrollbar (on by default, section 6.1); under Links, whether the link under the pointer is tinted, and whether a tooltip says where it leads (both on by default, section 6.1); under Tooltips, how long the pointer rests on a comment or a link before its tooltip shows (a slider, 0 to 2 s, 0.3 s by default, section 6.5); under Motion, smooth zooming (on by default, section 6.1) and smooth scrolling to the next and previous page with the view bar's buttons (on by default, section 6.1), to the places bookmarks and links in the text lead to (on by default, section 6.2), to the next and previous match of a search (on by default, section 6.1) and to annotations picked in the list (on by default, section 6.5), a switch each. **Appearance:** the theme, as three segments with icons (System by default, Light or Dark), which the command "Switch between the light and dark theme" (View, without a default shortcut) also sets: to Light or Dark, the other of the two on screen; under Start screen, Recent files always under the tools (a switch, off by default: in a wide window they are beside the tools, section 6.1); under Visual adjustments, how the pages of every document look (section 6.1): Brightness, Contrast and Sepia (sliders, as in the viewer's panel), Invert colours and Greyscale (switches), all as the pages are by default, and **Reset**, which puts them back so (disabled while they are). They apply at once to every open document that has no adjustments of its own, in every window. **Annotations:** the author name new annotations get (empty: the user name of the system account); under New annotations, whether new text markup opens its comment (off by default, section 6.5) and whether new annotations take the last color given to an annotation, and the last opacity given to their type (on by default, section 6.5); under Colours, the 18 preset colours as the panel shows them, each a button that opens the colour picker to give that preset another colour (with a button there to put its default back; a changed preset is named by its hex value), **Restore colours**, which puts all the defaults back, and whether a colour button opens its panel when the pointer is on it (on by default, section 6.5). Only presets that differ from the defaults are stored (a preset's place and its colour), so a later version's new defaults reach the others. Under Annotation list, whether the list keeps the sort last picked for every document (on by default; the sort is stored with the settings, and turning this off forgets it, section 6.5). **Toolbars:** under Annotation toolbar, its placement (docked beside the pages by default, always shown, or floating over them) and where it is docked (at the top by default, in the bar above the pages, or at the left or the right of them, in a bar of its own; shown, but not changeable, while the toolbar floats); under Floating toolbar, which says they are also used in full screen, since full screen floats the toolbar (section 6.1), its edge (bottom by default, top, left or right) and whether it shows only while the pointer is within about 72 px of that edge, while it has keyboard focus, while a panel or list one of its controls opened is open, and for 1.5 s after a tool is picked (off by default), both changeable while the toolbar is docked too. At a side, docked or floating, the toolbar's controls are stacked (section 8). An install from before the toolbar was docked by default keeps it floating, as its app data says. Under Quick tools, whether their bar shows over selected text (on by default), then the tools, a switch each with the tool's icon from the bar before its name, shown but not changeable while the bar is off, which keeps them as they are. An install from before the bar had its own switch, with every tool switched off, has the bar off and the default tools. **Shortcuts:** every command that can have a keyboard shortcut (those of the menus but the Help menu's, and the annotation tools, with or without one by default), grouped as the menus are (File, Edit, View, Go to, Document, Annotation tools), then the keys with a fixed use (Esc, Del, F2, Shift+F10, the arrow-key combinations, Ctrl+Enter, Alt held in full screen), which are listed but can't be changed. A search field above filters the rows by command, group or key. Each shortcut is a chip whose key is a button: pressing it, then the new keys, changes it; Backspace removes it and Esc leaves it as it was (and does not close Settings). A command can have up to three: the plus button at the end of every row adds one (a command without any shows only the plus), and a shortcut that was added, not one the command came with, has an x on its chip that removes it. There is no reset per command; **Reset all…** puts back the ones every command came with, after asking. Keys another command has are refused with that command's name, and so are keys with a fixed use (the keys that move the focus or edit text, Shift or Ctrl+Shift or Alt+Shift with arrows, Enter, F2, Shift+F10, Alt+F4, Ctrl+A, V and X). A change applies at once, and menus and tooltips show the keys in use. Whatever its keys, a command runs while a text field has the focus only if it did before (saving, finding, zooming, tabs...) and its keys include Ctrl or Alt or are a function key, never keys that edit text there (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+Z...). Only shortcuts that differ from the defaults are stored (a command's name and its keys), so a later version's new defaults reach the others. The groups and rows are declared once in `src/lib/features/settings/schema.ts`, which the dialog renders (the shortcuts have a panel of their own, and their commands and defaults are in `src/lib/shortcuts.ts`); a new setting is a field in Rust's `Settings` plus a row there, and a new command with a shortcut is an entry in `SHORTCUT_GROUPS`. Each change applies at once and is stored in app data (typed text once typing pauses or the field is left); there is no Save button, and the dialog closes with its close button, Esc or its shortcut again.
- **About** (Help menu), centred: the app icon (96 px), the name, then the version and its author on one line ("Version 1.0.0 · by Mark Ingrem", `AUTHOR` in `src/lib/config.ts`), the name in the text's colour and in the accent under the pointer, opening the author's GitHub profile; then one paragraph with the AGPL notice and MuPDF's credit, and a "Source code on GitHub" button with the GitHub mark (Simple Icons) that opens the repository. Both open in the default browser: Rust opens them from addresses it holds itself (`WebPage` in `ipc.rs`, kept equal to the frontend's by a test), so the webview neither goes online nor can have another page opened. The dialog takes the focus as it opens, with no ring around it; it closes with the button in its corner or Esc.
- **Updates** (ADR 0011): Lectrix asks GitHub for the latest release in the background once a day: after the startup files open when a day has passed since GitHub last answered a check (automatic or from the Help menu), and again each day while it stays open; a check that fails (offline) is tried again at the next start or within the hour. A newer one brings a floating notice, with a link to the changes it brings (What's new): Update, Not now (asked again at the next day's check) or Don't ask again (that version is never offered again; a later one is). Update downloads with a progress bar and Stop, and the download must pass the updater signature check. macOS and Linux then install it and offer Restart now or Later. Windows cannot replace the running app, so it offers Restart now (after the usual unsaved-changes prompts, in every window, the installer runs and opens Lectrix again) or Later (the installer runs when Lectrix closes). Failures of the automatic check are logged, never shown. **Check for updates…** (Help menu, above About) asks at any time, whether or not the automatic check is on, and offers a version skipped with Don't ask again: the notice shows "Checking for updates…" with a moving bar, then the same choices for a newer release, or a notification that Lectrix is up to date ("You have the latest version, 1.2.0."), or a plain error if GitHub can't be reached. While a notice is on screen it stays as it is; an update already downloaded and put off with Later brings back Restart now or Later instead of downloading again.
- **What's new** (Help menu, above Check for updates): the changelog (`CHANGELOG.md`) in a dialog, newest version first (the list takes the focus as it opens, so the keys scroll it), each version's changes under the parts of the app they touch (start screen, viewer and tabs, annotations, bookmarks, page labels, combining files, settings, installation and updates, general), with bold the only formatting. The first start after an update (a newer version than the one that started last; not a first install) says so until dismissed or followed: on the start screen, a block beside the app's name, over the second and third columns of tool cards and as wide as both, and as tall as the icon ("Updated to 1.1.0", then Dismiss, a button of text alone, and a See what's new button). The heading's row keeps that height without it, so nothing moves as it comes and goes: it grows from a little smaller as it fades in, and shrinks as it fades out, as a notification does (section 8.3); with documents open, a notification ("Lectrix was updated to 1.1.0", What's new). Before updating, the update notice's "See what's new" opens the same dialog with the changes since the installed version (from `latest.json`, in English) and Update at the bottom. A translation of the changelog (`CHANGELOG_RU.md`…) is shown for the app's language, and English for any version it lacks. How to write it: `docs/releasing.md`.
- **Releases** (ADR 0011, `docs/releasing.md`): a new version in `package.json` on `main` publishes a GitHub release once CI passes: Windows (the NSIS installer), Linux (AppImage, deb, and rpm for final versions) and macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel), with build provenance attestations and the updater's signed `latest.json`, and the version's part of `CHANGELOG.md` as the release's description. Versions follow semver, a pre-release as `1.0.0-beta.1`, and installers are built with `npm run bundle`.
- **Installers:** Windows has one, the NSIS setup program (ADR 0007, ADR 0020). It asks first (ADR 0015): "Install for anyone using this computer" (Program Files; preselected for an administrator) or "Install just for me" (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs`), as Tauri builds it, so an administrator answers a UAC prompt when it starts, updates included; an update keeps the earlier choice and folder, and silent installs take `/AllUsers` or `/CurrentUser`. It has a "PDF files" page after the folder page, "Open PDF files with Lectrix", checked by default. It registers Lectrix for PDFs (Open with, Default apps), with the Lectrix PDF icon (`lectrix-pdf.ico`, installed next to the app) for PDFs it opens; Windows asks which app to use at the next PDF, and the installer never takes over the default itself. For silent installs, `/NOPDF` leaves the registration out. It installs `LICENSE.txt` and the license notices. The setup program and the uninstaller it installs have the app's icon as their file icon (`installerIcon`, `uninstallerIcon`). Versions up to 1.1.0 also had an MSI, which installed for everyone: a copy installed from it updates through the setup program, which removes the MSI install first, asking nothing, installs into Program Files as the MSI did, and keeps whether it was registered for PDFs. On Linux the desktop file (Lectrix's own template, `src-tauri/linux/lectrix.desktop`, in the deb, the rpm and the AppImage) declares `application/pdf` and takes files (`%F`), so Lectrix is offered for PDFs (Open With, the default app); nothing makes it the default. The macOS bundle declares PDFs as a document type it edits, ranked Alternate, so Finder offers it in Open With without taking Preview's place (`tauri.macos.conf.json`). The Linux deb and rpm install AppStream metainfo (`src-tauri/linux/org.lectrix.pdf.metainfo.xml`, to `/usr/share/metainfo`) beside the desktop file and the icons: software stores (GNOME Software, KDE Discover) list an installed app from it, with the icon the desktop file names, and show a bare package without an icon when it is missing. The desktop file's category is Office (`bundle.category`).
